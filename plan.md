1. Product Overview
Product name: WONDERLAND
Tagline: A world of ideas, built one adventure at a time.
Portfolio owner: Achintha Bandara
Product type: Interactive, explorable 3D personal portfolio
Art direction: Colorful low-poly adventure
Primary experience: Explore a whimsical world of floating islands to discover Achintha's identity, professional experience, skills, projects, achievements, mentoring activities, and contact information.

WONDERLAND is a personal portfolio presented as a small, welcoming adventure game. Instead of browsing a conventional page of cards, visitors explore a colorful world made of distinct islands. Each island represents an important part of Achintha's professional and creative journey.

The experience should feel like a polished indie exploration game that happens to be a portfolio. It should be playful and memorable, but the professional information must remain clear, accurate, easy to find, and quick to access.

- Make it easy to access his CV, LinkedIn, GitHub, and email.

Target visitors
- Recruiters and hiring managers.
- Engineering leaders and technical peers.
- Potential collaborators and clients.
- Developers interested in enterprise AI and software engineering.
- Visitors exploring the portfolio out of curiosity.

3. World Concept
The world consists of a central Home Island surrounded by six themed islands. The islands are visible from the central hub and connected through bridges, paths, stepping stones, or other natural routes.

The world should feel like one coherent place, not seven unrelated scenes. Use a consistent visual language, landscape style, scale, lighting, and color palette across all areas.
World layout
The central island is the visitor's starting point. The other islands are arranged around it, with varied elevations and distances to create depth and a sense of discovery.

Conceptual arrangement:
- North: Achievement Peak
- Northwest: Career City
- Northeast: Skill Forest
- West: Innovation Lab
- East: Project Park
- South: Horizon Island
- Center: Home Island
This is a conceptual layout. The final arrangement should prioritize clear sightlines, attractive composition, intuitive paths, and comfortable exploration.

All major destinations must also be accessible through the world map and quick navigation. Visitors must never be forced to walk through the entire world to find important information.
4. Visual Identity and Art Direction
Overall style
Use a colorful, stylized, low-poly adventure-game aesthetic.

The world should be:
- Cheerful, welcoming, and imaginative.
- Visually rich without being cluttered.
- Whimsical but not childish.
- Distinctive and memorable.
- Calm enough for visitors to read and explore comfortably.
Visual characteristics
- Low-poly geometry and flat-shaded surfaces.
- Simple, exaggerated, recognizable shapes.
- Floating grass islands with visible rock undersides.
- Rounded hills, angular cliffs, and stylized mountains.
- Colorful geometric trees, oversized flowers, mushrooms, and rocks.
- Small houses, workshops, offices, laboratories, monuments, and landmarks.
- Stylized clouds, birds, rivers, waterfalls, and ocean.
- Bright daylight with soft shadows.
- Subtle environmental movement to make the world feel alive.
Avoid photorealism, detailed realistic textures, gritty cyberpunk visuals, dark horror aesthetics, and excessive neon.
Color palette
Use a consistent pastel-inspired palette:
- Meadow green: #80CFA9
- Sky blue: #75BDEB
- Sunshine yellow: #FFD27D
- Coral: #F5A6A6
- Lavender: #B9A1E8
- Warm cream: #F4E8C9
Use these as guiding colors, not rigid restrictions. Each island may have its own dominant color while remaining visually consistent with the overall world.
Interface style
The interface should feel like a natural extension of the adventure world.
- Warm cream-colored information panels.
- Rounded corners and soft shadows.
- Dark, readable text.
- Friendly illustrated icons.
- Wooden signboards, maps, journals, postcards, and notice boards.
- Simple, clear buttons and labels.
- Smooth, restrained transitions.
- Minimal on-screen controls.
Avoid making the interface look like a futuristic dashboard or a conventional corporate website.
